# Awesome-Fire-Department-Management

## Top Fire Department Management Platforms Ecosystem

**Curated List of SaaS Products & Open-Source GitHub Projects**

*Focused on Fire RMS, Incident Reporting, CAD, Scheduling, Training, Inventory, Pre-Plans & NFIRS/NERIS Compliance*

**Last updated: August 2026**



This repository tracks notable **SaaS platforms** and **open-source projects** for **Fire Department Management**. These tools help fire and emergency services agencies manage incident records, dispatch, personnel, apparatus, training, inspections, inventory, and regulatory reporting (NFIRS, NERIS, and related standards).



**Examples** include ESO Fire RMS, Firehouse Software, ImageTrend Elite, Emergency Reporting, Vector Solutions TargetSolutions, Operative IQ, First Due, StreetWise CAD, FirePrograms, and StationSmarts (the category leaders).



**Open-source emphasis**: Fully featured commercial-grade open-source fire RMS platforms are limited. This section is expanded with the strongest available open-source CAD/RMS systems, personnel and shift tools, and related emergency management projects that volunteer and smaller departments can self-host.



Contributions welcome! Open a PR to add/update entries. Keep descriptions factual and link to official sites.

## Open-Source GitHub Projects

- **[Tickets CAD (Open ISES)](https://openises.sourceforge.net/)**  

  Long-running free and open-source Computer Aided Dispatch system designed for organizations with limited budgets, including volunteer fire departments. Supports incident management, mapping, unit tracking, and messaging.



- **[Resgrid](https://github.com/resgrid)**  

  Open-source Computer Aided Dispatch (CAD), personnel, shift management, AVL, and emergency management platform that powers Resgrid.com. Includes core, dispatch, and responder applications.



- **[Lavra Response](https://github.com/lavrahq/response)**  

  Open-source MDT, RMS, CAD, and departmental management platform (originally oriented toward gaming/role-play communities but containing transferable CAD/RMS concepts).



- **[Older Fire Department Management System projects](https://github.com/)**  

  Legacy open-source efforts aimed at logging and tracking daily fire department data (personnel, apparatus, incidents).



- **[Fire simulation and engineering tools](https://github.com/)**  

  Open-source fire modeling and calculation libraries useful for training, pre-planning, and research (e.g., EMBRS and related simulators).



- **[Open fire-safety calculation engines](https://github.com/)**  

  Transparent, tested fire engineering methods and libraries that support engineering and pre-plan workflows.



- **[GIS and mapping integrations](https://github.com/)**  

  Open tools built on Leaflet, OpenStreetMap, or QGIS that departments adapt for response mapping and pre-incident planning.



- **[Scheduling and personnel open tools](https://github.com/)**  

  Self-hosted shift and certification trackers that can be customized for fire department staffing needs.



- **[ICS form and documentation helpers](https://github.com/)**  

  Open implementations of Incident Command System forms and digital checklists.



- **[Community emergency management platforms](https://github.com/)**  

  Broader open-source emergency management systems that include modules adaptable to fire operations.



### Additional Strong Open-Source Options

- Self-hosted inventory and asset trackers for apparatus and equipment.

- Training record systems built on open LMS platforms.

- Custom NFIRS/NERIS data validation scripts and reporting pipelines.

- Mobile data terminal prototypes and open PWA responders.

- Integration examples connecting open CAD systems to radio or messaging gateways.



**Frameworks for building custom systems**: Start with **Tickets CAD** or **Resgrid** for core dispatch and records capabilities, add open GIS layers for mapping, layer a simple personnel/scheduling module, and generate required regulatory reports via scripts or open BI tools. Keep all data on infrastructure you control. This approach is especially valuable for volunteer and smaller career departments that need functional tools without high recurring license costs.

## Disclaimer

- This is a **community-curated** list — not exhaustive and not an endorsement.

- Fire department systems handle life-safety, legal, and compliance-critical data. Open-source tools can provide valuable capabilities at low cost but require thorough testing, proper configuration, backup strategies, and validation against current reporting standards (NFIRS, NERIS, state requirements) before operational use.

- Always consult local regulations, mutual-aid agreements, and qualified public-safety technology advisors.
